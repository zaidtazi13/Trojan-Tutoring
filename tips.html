Trojan Tutors' Top Tips

Revision strategies, exam technique and application advice from our tutors — built from real experience and designed to help you study with more purpose.

Tips by stage & goal

Study smarter

Small changes in how you revise can make your study time much more useful.

KS3

Build the foundations first

Focus on understanding the core idea before trying to memorise detail. Explain a topic in your own words, then use short questions to check what has actually stuck. Build confidence by revisiting key skills regularly, correcting mistakes as you go and making sure each new topic connects back to what you already understand.

GCSE

Use the specification, practise and revisit

Use your exam-board specification as a checklist across your subjects. Identify gaps, practise with exam questions and mark schemes, record mistakes and return to weaker areas until you can answer them confidently.

A-LEVEL

Turn the specification into your revision plan

Work systematically through the specification, connect topics rather than learning them in isolation, and use mixed exam questions to test whether you can apply your knowledge. Keep track of weak areas and deliberately revisit them later.

UCAT

Review your method, not just your score

Timed practice matters, but improvement comes from reviewing why questions went wrong. Track recurring weaknesses and adjust your approach before completing another large question set.

MEDICINE INTERVIEWS

Don't memorise a script

Prepare structures, examples and key principles, then practise responding naturally. Strong interview answers show how you think, communicate and reflect rather than how well you can recite a paragraph.

DENTISTRY INTERVIEWS

Reflect on your experiences

Know what you learned from work experience, volunteering and wider reading. Be ready to explain how an experience changed your understanding of dentistry, teamwork, communication or patient care.

STUDY SKILLS

Revisit weak points

Don't mark a topic as “done” after one good session. Record weak areas and deliberately return to them in later revision. Spaced retrieval helps show whether the knowledge has genuinely lasted.

EXAM TECHNIQUE

Turn mistakes into your revision list

Keep a mistake log. Every time you lose a mark, record why it happened — missing knowledge, weak terminology, misreading the question or exam technique — then revisit the question later until you can answer it confidently.

These tips are a useful starting point, but our lessons go further. Tutors help students apply the techniques to their own subjects, identify where marks are being lost, practise with targeted questions and revisit weaker areas so the strategy becomes part of how they actually study and perform.

The Trojan approach

The Red, Amber & Green Specification Method

Turn your exam-board specification into a clear revision map based on how well you genuinely understand each point, then use that map to decide exactly what to revise next.

1

Go through the spec

Read every specification point carefully and make sure you understand exactly what the exam board expects you to know, explain and apply in questions.

2

Label it

Mark each point green if you know and understand it well, amber if you partly understand it, or red if you are not confident yet.

3

Target amber

For amber topics, go straight into targeted exam questions to expose the gaps, improve your exam technique and strengthen how confidently you apply the content.

4

Repair red

For red topics, first revisit the content until the core idea makes sense, then complete targeted questions to check whether your understanding now holds up.

5

Revisit mistakes

Return to questions you got wrong after a few days and attempt them again without looking at the answer, checking that you have genuinely learned from the mistake.

Keep updating the colours as you improve. The aim is to turn reds into ambers and ambers into greens, while still revisiting green topics so they stay secure.
