How progress tracking works

A continuous progress record — not a vague update every few weeks.

Your child’s tutor uses each lesson to build the next one. Our parent portal helps us keep that process visible to parents.

01

Lesson takes place

The tutor teaches the planned topic, checks understanding and notices where the student is secure or still struggling.

02

Tutor records the lesson

After the session, the tutor adds useful notes to the parent portal: what was covered, what went well, weak points, and what should be reinforced next.

03

Parents review the update

Parents can review the lesson history so they know what their child has actually worked through and where support is still needed.

04

Weak points carry forward

Identified gaps are not forgotten. The tutor uses previous notes to revisit them through recap, retrieval practice, questions or further teaching.

What you will see

Your child’s tuition, organised in one place.

Families can use the parent portal to access lesson notes and shared resources, and to keep track of lesson information outside the session. Trojan Tutoring will use the notes deliberately as a progress trail so parents can understand both what has been taught and what still needs attention.

Example progress note

GCSE Chemistry · Rates of Reaction

Covered

Collision theory, factors affecting rate and graph interpretation.

Strong

Confident explaining temperature and concentration.

Weak point

Needs more practice linking surface area to collision frequency.

Next lesson

5-minute retrieval recap, then exam-style rate graphs.

Who identifies the weak points?

Your Trojan tutor does. The parent portal is the system we use to organise and share the record. The tutor assesses the student during the lesson, identifies strengths and weaknesses, records the important points and uses them to shape future sessions.

Why this matters for parents

You do not have to wait for a general end-of-term update. You can follow what has been covered lesson by lesson and see whether previously identified weak areas are being revisited and strengthened.
